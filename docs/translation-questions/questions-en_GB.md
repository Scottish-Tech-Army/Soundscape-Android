# Soundscape in UK English — a few questions

> Email your answers to **soundscapeAndroid@scottishtecharmy.support**, with the language in the subject line.


Hello, and thanks for taking a look.

The app is written in American English, with a UK English layer on top. We'd
like to know where that layer still sounds American to you. **You don't need
to know or install the app:** each question says when you'd hear the text and
what it says now.

## What is Soundscape?

Soundscape is a free phone app for blind and partially sighted people. It's used
while walking, with headphones, often with the phone in a pocket. It doesn't
give turn-by-turn directions like a satnav ("turn left"); instead it says out
loud what's nearby, so people can orient themselves.

A few terms that come up:

- **Callout**: a short spoken message about something you're passing, such as
  "Café", "Pavement next to High Street" or "Heading north along High Street".
  It's heard in 3D audio, from the direction the thing is in.
- **Audio beacon**: when you pick a destination, a steady, repeating sound plays
  in your headphones from that direction. As you turn, the sound moves with you,
  so you can walk there by ear.
- **Markers** and **routes**: saved places, and a sequence of them the beacon
  leads you through one by one.

Blind users operate their phones with a **screen reader** (TalkBack on Android,
VoiceOver on iPhone), which reads every button and every piece of text aloud.
So the app's text is nearly always *heard* rather than read, often over traffic
noise. What matters most is that it's short, clear and natural to listen to.

There's more detail on these terms
[on this page]({{ "/developers/translation-terminology.html" | relative_url }}).

## How to reply

Just reply by email, quoting the question number ("Q1: I'd say…"). You don't
need to answer everything. If something already sounds right, a quick "fine"
helps too.

---

### Q1 — "Intersection" or "junction"?

**When you hear it:** constantly while walking, whenever you approach a place
where roads meet.

**What it says now:** "Approaching intersection", "Intersection in 20 metres".

**What we're unsure about:** UK pedestrians usually say "junction", but
Microsoft's original UK English kept "intersection" too, and changing it would
touch every crossing callout.

**The question:** would you say "junction"? Does it matter to you?

### Q2 — "Store" or "shop"?

**When you hear it:** in help text and general descriptions, for example "see a
list of nearby grocery stores".

**What we're unsure about:** "store" is American; "shop" is what most UK
speakers would say. (App Store and Play Store stay as they are.)

**The question:** does "store" jar?

### Q3 — Anything else American?

Words, spellings or phrases that sound American to a UK ear: "sidewalk",
"transit", "downtown", that kind of thing.

### Q4 — Anything else?

Anything that's unclear, too long, or awkward to listen to.

Thanks very much!
