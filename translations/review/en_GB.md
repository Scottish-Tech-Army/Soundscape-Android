# Soundscape in UK English — a few questions

Hello, and thanks for taking a look.

Soundscape is an app for blind and partially sighted people: as you walk, it
tells you out loud what's around you. The app is written in American English,
with a UK English layer on top. We'd like to know where that layer still sounds
American to you.

**How to reply:** just reply by email, quoting the question number ("Q1: I'd
say…"). You don't need to answer everything. If something already sounds
right, a quick "fine" helps too.

Nearly everything is **read aloud by a speech synthesiser**, often while
you're walking, so what matters most is how it *sounds*.

---

**Q1 — "Intersection" or "junction"?**
When you reach a place where roads meet, the app says things like
"Approaching intersection" and "Intersection in 20 metres". Would you say
"junction"? Does it matter to you?

**Q2 — "Store" or "shop"?**
In general descriptions the app sometimes says "store" (for example "a
store you pass") where you might say "shop". Does it jar?

**Q3 — Anything else American?**
Words, spellings or phrases that sound American to a UK ear: "sidewalk",
"transit", "downtown", that kind of thing.

**Q4 — Anything else?**
Anything that's unclear, too long, or awkward to listen to.

Thanks very much!
